2026/06/29

  • We were working on the [Darce](). Now I've only really seen this in the MMA, and I can't lie -- this is definitely one of the coolest submissions.
    Firstly, I still don't understand how this really works; I know it isn't a choke in the traditional sense of cuttin off the air -- althought it certainly can be -- the more faithful and effective approach is to cut off the blood supply of the Carotid artery. One thing I struggle with is how do you cover both sides? From the pov of the arm that wraps around: the farther side is covered by the forearm, but what about the near side? Is the bicep responsible. Some knowledge of the anatomy would surely help. I'm also curious as to how to use the 'off-hand' correctly. In my head the point is to use that for leverage. I still don't understand the squeezing motion at all.
  • Next we simulated the following positon: the defender is in turtule, the attacker has a 'head and arm grip'-- I don't know if this is the correct name, but essentially my left hand runs through the defender's armpit, across the chest and around the neck. From there on, you grab a near side [cradle](), get you head in the "pocket"-- the space between their armpit and hip -- and sort of bulldoze them over. From there you switch your grip to a Darce and complete the submission.
    The things I struggled with were (1) Using my head and toes as leverage effectively (2) making the switch of hands smooth (3) as mentioned before: finishing the choke. I also have to figure out what the effective positions for the finsih are -- I know it's common to be on your hip and 'crunch' but you can also just stay upright I think.
  • Then I did a few rounds of open mats.
    (1) Getting out of a position where the person has be in a [closed gaurd]()-- or well, in this case he simply had his legs wrapped around my body, didn't even use his hands the whole round. I was trying to use my hands and elbows as a wedge, but was later informed that that isn't a good approach; I shouldn't even use my hands at all, but should look up, keep my spine rounded, do not lean forward (lest I get sweeped), and try and 'expand my body' -- almost like you do when you [breathe through your Diaphragm]() during something like a sqaut.

(2) We started in a standing position, and I just got some tips about being aware of a [neck snap](), feet positioning and got a primer on using [overhooks]() and [underhooks](). Cool guy.

2026/06/27

  • Started with establishing some shin-to-shin contact. I see that this is usually a thing from the defender's perspective, but in our case, the top starts with one leg outside the defender's leg and one inside, and the idea is to put constant pressure on the defender's leg that is stuck in between, by first the outside leg, and then switching over to the inside to eventually get both legs past and pass. One thing I noticed was how constant that shin-to-shin contact was by my partner when they were passing while I let it off multiple times in the same attempt.
  • This led nicely into a knee cut pass. Well, the nicities weren't for me. But I think this is such a fundamental technique that it is a lifelong undertaking and I haven't had enough reps in to be even talking about it.
  • Then we did some rounds where the bottom gets to choose their positon -- mainly between a single leg x or del-hiva -- and the goal for the other person is just to break free from that contact. I definitely am very narrow minded in this case, from both positions to be honest. If I'm playing bottom, then I get too bogged down trying to retain the same grip I started with and if I'm top, I'm really just flapping around hoping I'm sweaty enough to slip by.
  • And lastly we practiced something which I thought was really fun: inverting from rev del-hiva. I have slightly longer legs so this is something I'm def looking to get better at.
